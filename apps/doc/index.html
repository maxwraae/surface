What

Make protein as accessible as DNAfdsfsdfs.

Today, ordering DNA is trivial — type a sequence, pay a few cents per base, get it in two days. Protein is nothing like that. Protein costs $300–500 per construct, takes weeks to months, requires a full lab and years of bench intuition, and fails more often than it works. The cost shapes the science: people study what they can afford to make, not what matters most.

We make protein like coffee. Sequence in, protein out. Fast, cheap, anyone can do it, zero expertise required.

Concretely, in order:

  1. Fast. Protein next day, not next month. We use mRNA transfection — skip cloning entirely. IVT makes the mRNA in hours, cells translate it immediately. Under 24 hours from sequence to purified protein.

  2. Cheap. $1 instead of $1,000. Automate the protocol end-to-end. Own the inputs — the platform makes the enzymes that make the mRNA that makes the proteins. The flywheel: every output feeds the next input.

  3. Open. Anyone can do it, not just us. A machine or kit that lives on someone else's bench. They don't need us.

  4. Easy. Type a sequence, get a protein. Room temperature. Ready to use. Plugs straight into experiments. If something will fail, the system tells you before you waste time.

The order is load-bearing. You can't make it cheap until it's fast. Can't make it open until it's cheap. Can't make it easy until it's open.

The chain

Protein production is a state chain. The material transforms at each step — what matters is what it is at each point, not the sub-steps inside each transition.

Code → DNA → mRNA → Cell → Protein → Package → Ship

Code. A sequence on a screen. Amino acids or nucleotides. Digital. This is where it starts — someone types what they want.

DNA. A physical molecule. The template. Either ordered from a vendor (days) or synthesized/assembled in-house (hours). Carries the T7 promoter, UTRs, coding sequence, poly-A tail — everything the IVT reaction needs.

mRNA. The instruction. Made from DNA by IVT. Inside this arrow: reaction, cleanup (remove DNA template, enzymes, dsRNA), QC. What comes out is a clean transcript the cell can read.

Cell. The factory. mRNA goes in via transfection (lipofection, electroporation). The cell's ribosomes read the mRNA and start making protein. Universal machinery — any cell, any protein.

Protein. The product. Inside the cell (or secreted). Has to be harvested, lysed, purified — isolated from everything else the cell contains. This is where most failures happen: misfolding, aggregation, low yield, lost activity.

Package. The deliverable. Buffer exchange, concentration, formulation, QC (identity, purity, activity). Room temperature if possible. Ready to use — no prep needed on the other end.

Ship. It leaves. Envelope, not dry ice. Shelf, not freezer. Arrives and goes straight into an experiment.
